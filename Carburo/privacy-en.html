PRIVACY POLICY

Your diary is local. Connections are explained here.

Last updated: 26 October 2026 · Data controller: Giuseppe Chirico

Italiano · English · Deutsch · Español · Français · Nederlands · Português

At a glance: Carburo keeps your diary on your device and does not require an account to use it. Some features contact external services: searches and maps, text recognition, crash reporting, the optional leaderboard and Android backup. Crash reporting is on by default and can be disabled; the leaderboard and trip recording are off by default.

Data stored locally

The app’s database and private storage hold vehicles (including registration plate and nickname), refuels, charges, expenses, odometer readings, consumption, reminders, notes, documents and photos, fuel vouchers (network, value, code, PIN, expiry and uses), trips, routes, parking locations and any coordinates. Your history is not uploaded to a developer server; exceptions for online features and backup are described below.

Optional online features

Recipients receive the requests described and normal connection data, including your IP address. Coordinates may be precise or approximate depending on permission and the selected point.

Italy: MIMIT (www.mimit.gov.it) supplies national station, price and regional-average files, filtered on your device without sending your location. For current prices, MIMIT Osservaprezzi (carburanti.mise.gov.it) receives area coordinates and radius, up to 10 km.

France: prix-carburants.gouv.fr through data.economie.gouv.fr receives coordinates and radius. Austria: E-Control (api.e-control.at) receives coordinates and fuel type. Germany: Tankerkönig (creativecommons.tankerkoenig.de) receives coordinates, radius and an API key. Spain: Geoportal Gasolineras (sedeaplicaciones.minetur.gob.es); Portugal: DGEG (precoscombustiveis.dgeg.gov.pt); Q8 catalogue (servicelocator.q8.it): national data is downloaded and filtered locally without sending your location.

Overpass/OpenStreetMap (overpass-api.de) receives coordinates and radius to find stations, charge points and brands. Open Charge Map (api.openchargemap.io) receives the selected charge point’s coordinates and an API key for details.

Maps load images from tile.openstreetmap.org: OpenStreetMap receives tile indices, which identify the displayed area, and the app’s identification. If you request a route to a station, OSRM (router.project-osrm.org) receives start and destination coordinates. If you open external navigation, Google Maps, Waze or your chosen app receive destination coordinates and, for a map pin, the place label; their own policies apply.

When you submit a place search, Nominatim (nominatim.openstreetmap.org) receives your search text and any selected country. If you enable “Place names in trips”, Android Geocoder (Google on devices using its service) receives start and end coordinates once when saving to return place names; this option is off by default.

For vehicle searches, RDW (opendata.rdw.nl) receives a Dutch registration plate, data.gov.il an Israeli plate and NHTSA vPIC (vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov) the VIN. If configured in your app version, Carburo’s managed plate service receives the plate, country and an anonymous installation identifier for rate limiting. Italian insurance and environmental-class checks open the Portale dell’Automobilista (www.ilportaledellautomobilista.it) in a WebView: the portal processes data you enter, such as the plate and security check, under its own policy.

For exchange rates, the ECB (www.ecb.europa.eu) supplies a daily file without receiving your selected currency or transaction data.

Location

You can deny permission and enter data manually. Coordinates saved in entries stay in your diary and backups; online searches, maps and price alerts may transmit a location as described here. When enabled, Trips records after a trip starts, with a visible notification; automatic Bluetooth start can start it with the app closed and requires background location. Route points stay local: you can delete them and set their retention. Frequent places, toll and parking links and trip costs are derived on your device; toll recognition uses data included in the app. Names returned by Geocoder stay in your diary and backups.

Vehicle photos and receipt recognition

Photos are copied to the app’s local storage; you can remove them without changing the gallery original. Receipt and display text is recognised on your device using Google ML Kit through Google Play services, which downloads the required model; Carburo does not send images to a server for recognition. However, Google’s SDK collects telemetry for diagnostics and usage analytics: device and app data, device and per-installation identifiers, performance, API configuration and input/output sizes, feature version, events and error codes. This telemetry is separate from Sentry and is not disabled by the crash-reporting switch. See Google’s ML Kit disclosure.

Crash reporting (on by default, can be disabled)

If configured in your installed version, it is on by default, with a notice on first launch. You can disable it from that notice or under More → Settings → Crash reporting; disabling closes Dart and native Android reporting. The recipient is Sentry (Functional Software, Inc., acting as data processor), in the project with storage in the EU region.

Reports may contain, when present in the SDK diagnostic event: event ID, timestamp, severity, platform, error type/message/mechanism, stack traces and threads; app version/build/package/name, environment, SDK/versions/integrations/packages, modules, debug images and identifiers for interpreting traces; manufacturer/brand/family/model/revision, CPU/architecture, screen/density/orientation, battery/charging/connectivity, memory/storage, emulator and boot time; Android name/version/build/kernel/root status, runtime and Flutter contexts, language/locale/timezone/accessibility, app start time, foreground state and permission diagnostics; technical trace/span identifiers for the individual event and origin and environment tags.

The code removes the original user object (ID, IP and geolocation), the device ID and app installation identifiers from crash contexts; it sets an empty IP in the event to prevent geolocation by Sentry. Technical event and build IDs do not identify the installation. Automatic sessions, screen history (breadcrumbs) and performance tracing are disabled; diary and photos are not attached. An IP address is still needed for the connection. The purpose is to fix errors; the legal basis is legitimate interest (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)) in app stability and security, with the right to object using the switch.

Local error log

Recent errors are also stored on your device, independently of Sentry. They may be included in the support email text, which you can review and send from your email app.

Provincial leaderboard (optional)

It is off by default. When enabling it, you choose a nickname, Italian province and fuel; Supabase, in a Carburo project in the EU, creates an anonymous identity with an ID and tokens, without email, phone number or password. Your chosen profile and “ottani” points earned after enabling are sent with award type, timestamp and a random event ID; not coordinates, plates, VINs, photos, costs or diary entries. Invite codes and friendships are also managed. Provincial participants and friends see your nickname, fuel and weekly points, not your province, invite code or friend list. Events older than the previous week (Rome time) are deleted every Monday.

To delete your profile, points, friendships and anonymous identity, disable the leaderboard in Settings: a connection is required; if it fails, the switch stays on so you can retry. Uninstalling does not perform this deletion. Tokens are local and excluded from Android backup; your local diary is unchanged.

Notifications

Reminders and summaries are local notifications, without developer push messaging. If you enable price alerts, a background check scheduled every 12 hours with a network connection sends the last refuel location and a 5 km radius to MIMIT Osservaprezzi, even with the app closed. Turning off price alerts prevents the check from running the search.

Backup and export

JSON and CSV files are created on your device and saved or shared to your chosen destination; if you choose a cloud service, its terms apply. Automatic local backup is on by default and can be disabled; it uses your chosen folder or Download/Carburo and retains the configured number of copies. The app does not encrypt these files.

The app also keeps a JSON export, date and backup destination name in its private restore folder. Only this folder is included in Android backup: cloud backup is allowed only with client-side encryption available on the device; Android manages transfer to the account’s backup service, normally Google. Android 12 and later also allow direct transfer to another device. The live database, preferences and tokens, receipt and vehicle photos and documents outside the restore folder are excluded. You can manage or disable backup in Android settings.

JSON backups include trip summaries, not route points. JSON exports retain references to receipt and vehicle photos, not the images. They include fuel voucher codes and PINs and photos of vouchers that are still usable; used or expired vouchers remain without photos. This also applies to the internal Android backup copy. Anyone with access to a JSON file can read and use this data: protect it and delete shared copies separately.

Retention, security and deletion

You can delete individual entries and routes in the app; clearing app data in Android settings or uninstalling removes private local data. External backups survive uninstallation: delete them from your chosen folder or service and manage Android copies separately to prevent a later restore. Delete your leaderboard participation before uninstalling, as described above. The listed services use HTTPS; this does not encrypt exported JSON files.

Children

Carburo is not designed specifically for children and does not knowingly collect children’s data.
